Practical 51.2 V architecture

What goes into a DIY 16S LFP battery?

Sixteen LiFePO4 cells make a common home-storage voltage. Here is what the numbers mean, which parts do which job and what to decide before buying anything.

Last reviewed: 16 August 2026

Why 16S is common

One LiFePO4 cell is usually described as 3.2 V nominal. Connect 16 in series and their voltages add to 51.2 V. That sits in the operating range used by many low-voltage inverter/chargers and rack batteries, so there is a large equipment ecosystem around it.

16 cells × 3.2 V = 51.2 V nominal

Series connection increases voltage while the amp-hour rating stays the same. The battery's actual voltage moves above and below 51.2 V as it charges and discharges, so the cell limits, BMS settings and inverter range must agree.

Nominal capacity is the simple voltage-times-amp-hours figure. Usable capacity is what you choose to use between the upper and lower limits. AC energy delivered to the home is lower again because the inverter and cables have losses.

Estimated AC-delivered energy = nominal kWh × usable fraction × inverter efficiency

What do I actually need?

  • Matched LFP cellsSixteen cells with known specifications and condition.
  • 16S-capable BMSCell-voltage and temperature monitoring, balancing and suitable current capability.
  • Main battery fuseChosen for the DC system, prospective fault current and connected equipment.
  • Battery disconnectA correctly rated way to isolate the battery without dismantling it.
  • Busbars and cablesShort, protected connections sized for current, temperature and voltage drop.
  • Enclosure and restraintProtection from contact and damage, with the cell support the manufacturer requires.
  • Temperature and current dataSensors or a shunt so the system can make sensible operating decisions.
  • Compatible inverter/chargerThe right voltage range, charge current, power and communication method.

BMS and inverter communication

The BMS keeps track of individual cells and tells the system when charging or discharging must slow down or stop. With closed-loop CAN or RS485 communication, a supported inverter can receive state of charge, voltage, temperature and charge/discharge limits. Without supported communication, some inverters can use carefully configured voltage-based control, but that is an equipment-specific design choice rather than a universal fallback.

A BMS is not the main fuse and it is not a substitute for a disconnect. Each part deals with a different job.
