Build a home battery plan, not a shopping list

Start with the energy and power your home needs. Then compare complete, compatible systems—not cheap cells or packs in isolation.

Last reviewed: 16 August 2026

Safety boundary: This site explains architecture, sizing and economics. It does not instruct unqualified people to open live EV packs, defeat interlocks or work on energised mains/high-voltage equipment. Grid-connected design and commissioning need competent assessment.

Four realistic routes

No route is universally best. Match integration effort and support to your competence, goal and risk tolerance.

Proprietary home battery

Best suited to households prioritising an integrated product and installer support.

Strength: product warranty and one ecosystem
Trade-off: higher cost and possible lock-in
Complexity: lower for the owner
Architecture: integrated AC or hybrid

Rack-mount 48/51.2 V LFP

Best suited to modular low-voltage systems using a documented inverter ecosystem.

Strength: modular and widely understood
Trade-off: compatibility still needs proof
Complexity: medium
Architecture: low-voltage DC

DIY 16S prismatic LFP

Best suited to competent builders who want transparent capacity and serviceable parts.

Strength: component control
Trade-off: designer owns containment and protection
Complexity: high
Architecture: custom 51.2 V nominal

Second-life EV pack

Best suited to experienced projects where reuse and large capacity justify the integration work.

Strength: potentially low battery-only £/kWh
Trade-off: health and controls uncertainty
Complexity: very high
Architecture: usually high voltage

AC energy flow

Monitoring, temperature sensing, suitable containment and a defined protective strategy sit across the chain. A BMS reports or limits battery conditions; it does not replace correctly rated over-current protection or a safe disconnect.

Size energy and power separately

Energy · kWhHow long the battery can supply energy.
Power · kWHow much load it can supply at once.
Battery energy10 kWh
Inverter limit3 kW
Requested load7 kW
ResultBattery cannot sustain the full load

For a 16-series LFP example, 16 × 3.2 V gives 51.2 V nominal; 51.2 V × 200 Ah ÷ 1,000 gives 10.24 kWh nominal.

Estimated usable kWh = nominal kWh × chosen usable fraction

Keep inverter losses and reserve margin separate so nominal, DC-usable and AC-delivered energy are not confused.

Compare complete costs

Include battery, BMS or controller, inverter/charger, protection, enclosure, cabling, monitoring and installation. For an EV pack also include transport, health testing, CAN interface, contactor/pre-charge integration and cooling where required. See the LFP versus second-life comparison.
