Battery communication and control

Stark CMR, LilyGo and other Battery-Emulator hardware

Battery-Emulator is the software. These boards provide the CAN interfaces and control outputs that let the software communicate with an EV battery and a supported inverter.

Last reviewed: 16 August 2026

More than two hardware routes

Stark CMR and LilyGo are the names most often discussed, but they are not the only boards supported by the project. The current hardware catalogue also includes BECom, Waveshare, DFRobot and specialist development-board options. The right choice depends on the CAN interfaces, control outputs and enclosure standard your particular battery and inverter need.

Stark CMR v2

A commercial DIN-rail controller built specifically for Battery-Emulator. It combines protected outputs, two CAN interfaces, CAN-FD, RS485 and switchable termination.

Project rating: newcomer friendly
Best for: tidy permanent control panels
Advantage: fewer add-on relays and boards
Trade-off: substantially more expensive

LilyGo T-2CAN

A lower-cost ESP32 development board with two isolated CAN interfaces. CAN-FD and extra functions depend on the exact model or add-on hardware.

Project rating: newcomer friendly
Best for: cost-conscious DIY development
Advantage: inexpensive and widely used
Trade-off: enclosure, outputs and connections need more work

BECom

An open-source DIN-rail design with three isolated CAN interfaces and RS485.

Project rating: newcomer friendly
Best for: multi-bus open-hardware builds
Advantage: three CAN channels
Trade-off: availability and assembly route need checking

Other supported boards

Waveshare ESP32-S3, DFRobot Edge101, 3LB, older LilyGo T-CAN485 and generic ESP32 boards cover different budgets and interface requirements.

Best for: specific interfaces or experimentation
Advantage: more hardware choices
Trade-off: support and ease of use vary considerably

What Stark CMR adds

  • Two CAN busesOne classic CAN and one CAN/CAN-FD interface keep battery and inverter traffic separate where required.
  • RS485 / ModbusUseful for supported inverters or batteries that communicate over RS485.
  • Protected outputsA 7 A continuous relay channel and three lower-current protected MOSFET channels can control suitable auxiliary loads.
  • DIN-rail formatDesigned for a cleaner control-panel installation than an exposed development board.
  • Termination switchesCAN and RS485 termination can be changed without modifying the board.
  • Emergency-stop inputThe v2 provides a dedicated signal input that can be used by Battery-Emulator’s equipment-stop function.

Stark CMR can simplify automatic pre-charge and contactor control, separate CAN buses and CAN-FD battery integrations. It does not make an unsupported battery and inverter compatible, and it does not replace HV protection, isolation monitoring or system-level design.

Choose from the interfaces backwards

  1. Find the exact battery in the project’s current supported-battery documentation.
  2. Find the exact inverter and note its voltage window and communication protocol.
  3. Check whether battery and inverter can share a CAN bus or require isolated channels.
  4. Check whether the battery uses classic CAN or CAN-FD.
  5. List the outputs needed for BMS power, pre-charge, contactors and equipment stop.
  6. Only then choose the controller board.

A cheap controller is not cheaper if it needs several extra interface boards, relays and hours of troubleshooting. Equally, Stark CMR can be unnecessary for a simple, proven combination that needs only two CAN channels and external control hardware you already have.
