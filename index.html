Illustrative UK example

A household using 4,000 kWh a year

Suppose a home has space for ten 440 W panels, giving a 4.4 kWp array. At an assumed yield of 900 kWh per kWp, estimated annual generation is about 3,960 kWh.

If 45% is used directly, around 1,782 kWh avoids grid import before battery shifting. The remaining generation is either stored or exported. Battery value depends on usage timing, losses, tariff spreads and installed cost.

UK solar and battery questions

Frequently asked questions

How many solar panels do I need in the UK?

It depends on annual electricity use, panel rating, roof space, orientation and local solar yield. The calculator estimates panel count and array kWp, then limits the result to the roof space you enter.

What size home battery do I need?

Battery sizing depends on how much electricity you use and when you use it. Solar surplus, off-peak charging, usable depth of discharge, power limits and backup goals also matter.

Is a solar battery worth it in the UK?

Not always. A battery can improve self-consumption or shift off-peak electricity, but losses and additional installed cost may worsen simple payback.

Can I include an electric car in a solar calculation?

Yes. Entering EV mileage adds estimated charging demand. Actual benefit depends on whether charging happens during solar generation or an off-peak window.

Can a battery charge overnight on a cheap tariff?

A compatible battery inverter/charger and tariff can make this possible. The result depends on the price spread, efficiency, charging hours and supplier terms.

Does the estimate replace an installer or electrical design?

No. Roof structure, shading, cable sizing, protection, product compatibility, DNO requirements and final electrical design require competent professional assessment.
