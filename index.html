Illustrative UK example

A household using 4,000 kWh a year

Suppose a home has space for ten 440 W panels, giving a 4.4 kWp array. At an assumed yield of 900 kWh per kWp, estimated annual generation is about 3,960 kWh.

If 45% is used directly, around 1,782 kWh avoids grid import before battery shifting. The remaining generation is either stored or exported. A battery can increase self-consumption, but its financial value depends on when the home uses energy, conversion losses, tariff spreads and the installed cost entered by the user.

UK solar and battery questions

Frequently asked questions

How many solar panels do I need in the UK?

It depends on annual electricity use, panel rating, roof space, orientation and local solar yield. The calculator estimates panel count and array kWp, then limits the result to the roof space you enter.

What size home battery do I need?

Battery sizing depends on how much electricity you use and when you use it—not annual kWh alone. Solar surplus, off-peak charging, usable depth of discharge, power limits and backup goals also matter.

Is a solar battery worth it in the UK?

Not always. A battery can improve self-consumption or shift off-peak electricity, but losses and additional installed cost may worsen simple payback. The calculator is allowed to show that storage does not improve the modelled economics.

Can I include an electric car in a solar calculation?

Yes. Entering EV mileage adds an estimated charging demand to household electricity use. Actual benefit also depends on whether charging happens during solar generation or an off-peak tariff window.

Can a battery charge overnight on a cheap tariff?

A compatible battery inverter/charger and tariff can make this possible. The financial result depends on the peak/off-peak price difference, battery efficiency, available charging hours and supplier terms.

Does the estimate replace an installer or electrical design?

No. It is an early-stage comparison. Roof structure, shading, cable sizing, protection, product compatibility, DNO requirements and final electrical design require competent professional assessment.
